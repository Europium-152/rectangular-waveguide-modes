# T2 report — coupled local-mode equations of tapers with curvature

Card: `tasks/T2-taper-coupling.md` · Branch: `wave-1/T2-taper-coupling` · Role: Theory

## What was done

* Derived the coupled local-mode equations of a rectangular guide with $a(z)$, $b(z)$, centre offsets $u_0(z)$,
  $v_0(z)$ and planar curvature $\kappa(z)$, from the T0 transverse Maxwell equations, keeping every boundary term:
  the moving-domain (Leibniz) term and the tilted-wall term $hE_z = -wE_n$. Result (exact, infinite system):
  $dV/dz = -iZI + N^TV$, $dI/dz = -iYV - NI$, with $N_{pq} = \iint_{S(z)} e_p\cdot\partial_ze_q$ (domain: the
  cross-section of the projecting mode) and $Z$, $Y$ the T0 curvature matrices at the local $a, b$ plus a diagonal
  $h_0 = 1 + \kappa u_0$.
* Closed forms for all TE/TM pairs, written with polarization vectors and four 1D integer matrices (no
  $1/(\chi_p^2-\chi_q^2)$ denominators); power-wave form; selection rules; analytic proofs of power conservation
  and reciprocity (every truncation).
* Specified the cutoff-crossing method for WP5 (GSM slices built directly from V–I Magnus propagators, mode list
  by the widest section, slice-boundary rules, port handling), with a working prototype.
* Small-slope (single-scattering) formulas, the turning-point reflection, and the bottle-taper explanation.
* Read Katsenelenbaum (1998) §1.3, §2.3 (pp. 35–41, 81–91), Butterweck & de Ronde (1968) pp. 95–97, Huang
  vol. II §11.14 and §11.17 (pp. 474–479, 489–494); comparison in section 11 of the note.

Deliverables: `docs/theory/T2-taper-coupling.md` (boxed formulas, method, validity limits);
`docs/theory/T2/t2_coefficients.py` (one function per coefficient, for the T2/T2b comparison);
`docs/theory/T2/check_closed_forms.py`, `check_exact_fields.py`, `check_solver.py` (checks, all pass);
`docs/theory/T2/exact_fields.py` (exact Maxwell fields in wedges, a straight guide in a curved frame, tilted guides);
`docs/theory/T2/vi_solver.py` (reference solver, prototype for WP5). No change to `src/` or `tests/`.

## Results (numbers from the scripts)

* Tilted-wall term and moving-domain term cancel exactly in the $V$ equation; in the $I$ equation the moving-domain
  term survives and is $\sum_qW_{pq}I_q$, $W = -N-N^T$. Taking the overlap derivative on the other domain gives
  $-N^T$, which differs from $N$ by 42 % and 79 % of $\max|N|$ in the two test geometries.
* Against exact Maxwell fields in seven configurations (E- and H-plane wedges, the H-plane one at three planes
  around the cutoff of TE$_{30}$, a straight guide in a curved offset frame, two tilted guides) the truncated
  equations converge: $V$ equation as $M^{-3}$ (residual 1e-5–1e-4 at 121–220 modes), $I$ equation as $M^{-1}$;
  the variants without the tilted-wall or the moving-domain term do not converge (residual O(1)).
* GSM-slice solver: single mode through cutoff (Airy) exact to 1.6e-9, linear taper (Bessel) to 2.6e-9; reciprocity
  3.9e-15, unitarity 8.8e-15 with κ, $a'$, $u_0'$, $b'$ and evanescent modes; total reflection at a cutoff plane
  $1-|S_{22}| = 2.1\times10^{-5}$, phase within 0.013 rad of $i\,e^{-2i\int\beta}$; first-order formula within
  0.26 % of the full conversion for a gentle taper.
* **Truncation converges only as $O(1/M)$**, with a constant proportional to the wall slope: for an H-plane wedge
  with $a' = 0.41$ the low-order GSM blocks change by 3.6e-2, 1.7e-2, 8.5e-3, 4.3e-3 for $M$ = 8→16→…→128
  (`study_convergence.py`). Cause: Gibbs behaviour of $H_t$ (the true $H_n = (w/h)H_z$ on a tilted wall is not
  representable by the local modes). Richardson extrapolation in $1/M$ fixes it in practice: the extrapolated
  values differ by 2.1e-3, 2.9e-4, 5.8e-5 between successive doublings. For a smooth taper with slope ≤ 0.27 the
  TE$_{10}\to$TE$_{30}$ conversion changes by only 2.4e-5 between $f_c$ = 3 and 4.

## Open issues

1. **$O(1/M)$ convergence of the truncated equations** (above). WP5 should report two mode counts and the
   Richardson extrapolation; the WP4 staircase comparison must be made at converged (or extrapolated) mode counts.
   A reformulation that adds a boundary-layer term to the $H$ expansion could restore fast convergence; not
   attempted (time box).
2. Curvature combined with moving $v$-walls ($\kappa\neq0$ and $b'\neq0$ together) is derived but not checked
   numerically: no exact field was found for that geometry. κ with moving $u$-walls is checked.
3. The TM turning-point phase ($-i$) is derived but not checked numerically (TE: checked).
4. Katsenelenbaum's coefficients (eqs. 2.95, 2.98) were not compared term by term: the normalization of his
   eigenfunctions (eq. 1.16) is not legible in the scan. Structure and the reflection density agree.
5. The Riccati alternative (§8.3) is specified but not prototyped.

## Conventions issues

* §6 says transfer matrices are only for propagating modes. The method of §8.2 never forms a global transfer
  matrix: each short slice's GSM is built directly from its V–I propagator, with bounded evanescent growth. I read
  this as compliant; WP5 should not call `from_transfer_matrix` with evanescent modes.
* §3: "Coupled-mode (transfer-matrix) solvers use cutoff_factor = 1." A taper with a cutoff crossing is a
  coupled-mode solver that needs evanescent modes, and its internal mode list must be fixed by the *widest*
  section. WP5 must state its own default under the "solvers that need evanescent modes" clause.
* §3 basis order (by cutoff) changes along a taper when $a/b$ varies (e.g. TE$_{20}$/TE$_{01}$ swap at $a = 2b$).
  Internal slices must use one fixed order (`sort=False`); only the end ports use the standard order. Not an
  error, but worth a sentence in WP5.

## For other cards

* **WP5**: everything needed is in §3, §4, §8 of the note; `t2_coefficients.py` and `vi_solver.py` are references
  (pure Python loops, slow above ~50 modes; vectorize).
* **WP4**: the overlap between two adjacent cross-sections must be taken on the domain of the projecting mode to
  converge to $N$ (§2.5); the other side gives $-N^T$.
* **T1**: the cancellation of §2.2 uses $E_s = 0$ on the wall; with impedance walls on a taper the boundary terms
  must be redone.
* **T3**: the same machinery (Leibniz term + tilted-wall term with a normal velocity $w$ from the rotation) applies
  to twists.
* **WP7 / verifiers**: `exact_fields.py` provides exact Maxwell fields in tapered, offset and curved-frame
  geometries; projecting them gives local-mode amplitudes that any taper solver must map into each other.
