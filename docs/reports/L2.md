## Method

Pass a and pass b differ in raster, calibration and extraction; both were done by L2 (one agent, two independent
procedures, not two agents as in L1):

* **Pass a**: 400 dpi `pdftoppm` render. Calibration: Alimenti from the major ticks (tick centroids, least
  squares) with the axis tilt (≤ 0.0018) corrected as a shear; Kotik from the five vertical gridlines (each fitted as
  a straight line, rms ≤ 0.27 px, kd piecewise linear between them at the row of the point) and the 1.0 and 0.75
  lines followed with a running median (the frame wobbles by ±1.6 px). Extraction: a ridge follower (steps along
  the tangent, ink centroid on the normal profile, direction search at sharp turns) in hand-seeded segments; dashed
  lines from the dash components near a coarse guide, cut into 3 px slices; markers from the maxima of the ink
  distance transform that stand out from the local stroke half-width.
* **Pass b**: the embedded 300 ppi page scans (`pdfimages`). Calibration: Alimenti from major plus minor ticks (minor
  ticks identified against the major fit), no tilt correction; Kotik from straight-line fits of all gridlines, kd
  as a quadratic through the five gridline positions, y linear between straight 1.0 and 0.75 lines. Extraction:
  column runs (ink-weighted centre of the vertical run nearest a prediction or a coarse hand-placed guide);
  markers by correlating the ink with a disc of the symbol radius around positions placed by eye.
* **Merge** (`scripts/l2_merge.py`, imports `merge_line`/`merge_markers` of L1's `merge_ab.py`): grid in x, mean of
  a and b, uncertainty max(|a-b|/2, u_a, u_b), single-pass stretches doubled, no bridging of gaps longer than the
  per-file limit nor of reflection zeros (gap ends within 6 dB of the bottom frame). Flags (|a-b| > combined
  reading uncertainty): 16 of the 2170 merged rows, all explained in the file headers (steep flanks,
  doubled strokes, the last BCMM diamond, the faint end of Kotik's solid line).
* **Kotik drafting term**: the dashed curve of Kotik Fig. 3 is a known formula. Both passes find it off the formula
  by -0.010 at kd = 12 and +0.004 for kd > 18 (rms 0.005, max 0.011), which no axis shift explains (a kd shift
  would have to be -0.45 at kd = 12 and +1 at kd = 25; the y calibration is good to 0.0007). This is how accurately
  the figure was drawn, so 0.005 is added in quadrature to the reading uncertainty of the rigorous curve.

`scripts/l2_check.py --alimenti <pdf> --kotik <pdf> --out <scratch>` renders the pages, overlays the merged points
(PNG files in the scratch folder only) and reports the residuals in 400 dpi pixels:

## Geometry conversions and doubts

**Alimenti et al. (1994), t.** The coordinator's note (t measured from the outer corner, d/a = (2 − t/a)/√2) does
not match the paper. On the inset of Fig. 4 (PDF p. 3, checked on a 3x zoom of the 400 dpi render; the native scan has 300 ppi) the two arrow heads of
the dimension t sit on the end of the 45° miter on the outer wall and on the thin vertical line that continues the
inner wall of the other arm. So t is the part of the outer wall left standing, the cut along each outer wall
measured from the removed outer corner is c = a − t (Ma et al. 1997's c), and

  **d/a = (1 + t/a)/√2** (CONVENTIONS.md §6, distance of the miter plane from the inner corner).

Supporting evidence: (1) the fully mitered bend of Fig. 3 (inset: miter from the outer-wall point opposite the inner
corner on one wall to the same point on the other) is t = 0, d/a = 1/√2, the optical mirror; t/a = 1 is the
unmitered corner of Fig. 2. (2) Here the zero frequency rises with t/a (Figs. 4, 5); in Ma et al. Fig. 11 it falls
with c/a, as it must if c = a − t. (3) t/a = 0.30 ↔ c/a = 0.70: zero at f/fc 1.29 here, 1.38 in Ma; t/a = 0.386 ↔
c/a = 0.614: two zeros (1.70-1.73 and 1.943) and the widest 30 dB band, like Ma's c/a = 0.631 (zeros 1.68 and 1.93,
the "39 %" band). Under the other reading t/a = 0.386 would be c/a = 0.386, a nearly unmitered corner with no zero
at all. Values: t/a = 0.30, 0.34, 0.38, 0.386 → d/a = 0.919, 0.948, 0.976, 0.980.

Doubts: (a) t is dimensioned on one wall only; the text says the miters are at 45°, so the cut is symmetric.
(b) Fig. 5 has no inset (the card mentions insets of Figs. 4-5); its t is the same t. (c) The guide's dimensions
are not given (f/fc only); for TE_n0 in an H-plane bend b does not matter below the TE01 cut-off. (d) The
quantitative match with Ma is not perfect: Fig. 2 (unmitered) agrees with Ma c/a = 0 to 0.14 dB rms (146 points,
none outside the combined uncertainty), but Fig. 3 (optical mirror) lies 0.5-1.0 dB above Ma's c/a = 1.0 (mean
+0.68 dB over 140 points; at f/fc 1.686: −10.80 dB here, −11.60 dB in Ma Fig. 11 and −11.63 dB in Ma Table 1), and
the zero for t/a = 0.30 is 0.09 lower in f/fc than Ma's c/a = 0.70. Both passes and the overlay check confirm the
reading of Fig. 3, so this is a difference between the two papers (or their models of the 45° wall), not a
digitizing error. WP3b/WP4b should not treat Alimenti Fig. 3 and Ma c/a = 1.0 as interchangeable before their own
results decide.

**Kotik & Sivov (1965).** Figs. 1-2 (PDF p. 3): the bend is a right angle; the mirror runs from the end of one
outer wall to the end of the other, i.e. along the diagonal of the square D (0 ≤ z ≤ d, 0 ≤ x ≤ d) where the two
guides cross; it passes through the intersection of the arm axes, so it is the optical mirror, **d/a = 1/√2**
(CONVENTIONS §6 plain miter). d is "the dimension of the waveguide" with α_n = πn/d, i.e. the guide width a, so
**kd = 2πa/λ**, and TE_n0 is cut off at kd = nπ. The field has only E_y (normal to the plane of the bend, parallel
to the mirror edge); their "H01" wave is TE10 of a rectangular guide in an H-plane bend.

Formula: the translation prints |B̃1|² = 1 − [7.4/(kd)^{2/3}]. The exponent must be 3/2: with 2/3 the value at
kd = 10 is −0.59, with 3/2 it is 0.766, where the dashed curve starts, and the dashed curve follows 1 − 7.4/(kd)^{3/2}
within 0.011 over its whole length. Validity stated: for kd > 6π the relative deviation from the rigorous solution
is at most 3 %. Remarks (p. 142): at kd ≈ 30 the wave loses only 3 % of its power (the figure: 0.971 at kd = 30);
Fig. 3 was plotted with attention to the critical frequencies kd = nπ and "no drastic changes" occur around them —
yet the curve has a sharp dip to 0.746 just above 4π (minimum at kd = 12.9 on the gridline scale, the fall starts at
12.1) and smaller ones just above 6π and 8π. The dashed curve is labelled |B̃1| in one sentence and |B̃1|² in the
formula; the plotted values are |B̃1|².

**Kotik x axis.** The gridlines are at kd = 10 (frame), 15, 20, 25, 30; the labels 4π, 5π, 6π, 7π, 9π, 10π are printed
between them without tick marks. Calibration from the gridlines; their spacing grows from 257 to 263 px (400 dpi)
across the figure (drawing or scan distortion), hence the piecewise map. Check against the labels (centre of each
printed label mapped through the gridline scale): numeric labels −0.09 to +0.16 kd, π labels −0.09 to +0.36 kd
(5π is pushed right, away from "15"); a single linear fit through all 11 label centres has an rms residual of 0.13 kd
(max 0.23 kd) and a slope 0.7 % from the gridline scale. Label placement scatter is therefore ±0.15 kd; the gridline
calibration itself is good to 0.02 kd (pass a) and 0.013 kd (pass b, including the quadratic's residuals), and the
two passes agree to 0.001 in |B1|².

## Kotik & Sivov's method (eqs. 2-10), in our words, for WP4b/WP7b

1. **Fields in the arms.** u = E_y obeys ∇²u + k²u = 0 with u = 0 on the walls and on the mirror. In the input arm
   (z < 0) u is the incident TE10 wave e^{−ih_1 z} sin α_1 x plus reflected modes A_n e^{+ih_n z} sin α_n x; in the
   output arm (x < 0) it is a sum of transmitted modes B_n sin α_n z times an exponential in x, with α_n = nπ/d,
   h_n = √(k² − α_n²), time factor e^{iωt} (for evanescent modes the root must be taken so that they decay away
   from the bend; the paper does not say). Doubt: eq. (3) prints B_n e^{−ih_n x} sin α_n z for the output arm, while
   eq. (6), which describes the same arm in the image system, has −B_n e^{+ih_n x} sin α_n(d − z), i.e. the other
   sign of the exponent (outgoing towards x → −∞ for e^{iωt}) and a factor (−1)^n; (3) looks like a misprint or uses
   another orientation of x. Only |B_n| is used in the paper.
2. **Image system.** A plane mirror on the diagonal of the crossing square is equivalent to removing the mirror and
   continuing both guides through the square: two crossed guides, fed in their two input arms by waves of opposite
   sign, so that u is odd about the diagonal and vanishes on it. The fields in the two image arms (x ≥ d and z ≥ d)
   are the mirror images, with a sign change, of the fields in the real arms: eqs. (4)-(5) for the image of the input
   and output arms, (6) for the output arm, (2) for the input arm.
3. **Green's theorem on the square.** On the boundary Γ of the square D, ∮_Γ (u_p ∂u/∂n − u ∂u_p/∂n) ds = 0 for any
   u_p that solves the same Helmholtz equation in D. They take the two travelling modes of the horizontal guide,
   u_p¹ = e^{−ih_p z} sin α_p x and u_p² = e^{+ih_p z} sin α_p x, p = 1, 2, … On the two sides z = 0 and z = d the
   integrals reduce, by orthogonality of sin α_n x, to terms in A_p (and B_p) times h_p d; on the sides x = 0 and
   x = d the fields are expansions in the other guide's modes, and the integrals are overlap integrals of
   sin α_n z with e^{∓ih_p z}, I_pn = α_n [e^{−ih_p d} − (−1)^n] / ((α_n² − h_p²) d) as printed.
4. **Linear system.** The two choices of u_p give, for every p, two equations, (9) and (10), linking A_p, B_p
   (diagonal terms h_p d (1 + δ_p1)) to sums over all n of h_n I_pn A_n and h_n I_pn B_n (with alternating signs
   (−1)^p, (−1)^{n+1}); the right-hand sides come from the incident wave (I_p1 terms and, in (10), a term
   2h_1 d e^{−h_p d} δ_p1 as printed). Symmetry about the mirror gives A_2n = B_2n, which halves the unknowns
   for the even modes.
5. **Truncation and output.** The infinite system is cut to the first 2N equations in A_1…A_N, B_1…B_N (4N real
   unknowns), N ≤ 18 on their computer; graphical accuracy needs all propagating modes plus one evanescent mode. The
   result is |B_1|², the TE10 power transmitted into TE10 of the output arm. Energy goes into reflection (A_n) and
   conversion to higher TE_n0 (B_n, n > 1).

For WP4b/WP7b: this is a mode-matching method on the crossing square with a Green's-theorem (reaction) projection
instead of a field match on a plane, and it needs no discretization of the oblique wall. It is exact only for the
optical mirror (the image argument needs the mirror on the diagonal of the crossing square); the footnote claims the
same for any bend angle (mirror on the bisector). The printed formulas of (9)-(10) and I_pn should be re-derived,
not copied: the scan's typography is poor (e.g. e^{−h_p d} in (10) is likely e^{−ih_p d}), and the translation has
at least one misprint (the exponent of the asymptotic formula).

## Uncertainties

Per point, in the files: max(|a − b|/2, u_a, u_b) (doubled for single-pass stretches), with u_a, u_b the reading
uncertainties (a quarter of the stroke width or 1 px, divided by the cosine of the tangent angle, plus the
calibration residuals and slope × x calibration error), plus the 0.005 drafting term for Kotik's rigorous curve.
Calibration residuals: Alimenti tick fits rms 0.0013-0.0020 in f/fc and 0.0025 dB (Fig. 2), 0.16 dB (Fig. 3,
uneven tick spacing in the print, ±3 px), 0.10 dB (Fig. 4), 0.008 dB (Fig. 6), 5e-5 in t/a and 0.0008 in f/fc
(Fig. 5); Kotik 0.005 kd and 0.0002 in |B1|² (pass a), 0.013 kd and 0.0007 (pass b). Per CONVENTIONS §8 these data
never set a tolerance tighter than their uncertainty.
